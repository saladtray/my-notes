
### Fate of Dictators 1875-2004
74% get ousted by elite rebellion
18% get ousted by public rebellion
8% get ousted due to foreign intervention

### Who Is The Elite
- Autocratic elites are those who occupy state positions
- They can leverage that influence to collectively maintain or depose the autocrat's rule 
- It's about political power, not wealth 
	- despite the economic sanctions on Russia, Putin did not suffer any consequences. Putin does not need to keep around the rich elite but rather those in the state who have political power. But also cannot alienate his elite, for if he loses power, he will end up in prison. Less likely to challenge to the elite. 
- ==The elite are the political elite== 

### Dictators and Autocratic Elites
- There are many kinds of autocratic elites: military generals, state officials, party leaders
- These elites can threaten to coup the dictator 
- **Strong** elites can make demands to dictators. Dictators accept to avoid coups. 

### Why Keep Elites Around? 
- No autocrat can rule alone
- They need elites to 
	- Run the state
	- Maintain the control over the population 

### Selectorate Theory
- Selectorate = regime elites 
- Reciprocal Accountability - the selectorate decides who rules, but the leader also decides who is part of the selectorate (think of selectorate as of the elite and the leader as the dictator or autocrat)
- **Key Idea**: We can explain the political dynamics across different autocracies by understanding the relationships between the elite and dictator 

### What Makes Elites Strong
Elites cannot rely on a constitutional arrangement or a political pact to protect themselves. Elite power resides in their ability to coordinate a challenge against the dictator
- e.g. coup, challenge of plans, succession
Challenges require **elite cooperation** 

### Elements of Elite Collective Action
Elite Power: The extent to which formal positions of power counterbalance dictators. 
Elite Cohesion: Number of distinct interests within the states and empowered actors outside of it. 
	- infighting between elites gives wiggle room to dictators
	- dictators can "divide and conquer" powerful elites to monopolize power 

### What Does Elite Power Look Like?
- Elites are powerful when they control spaces within the state
- When they control large organizations that provide political control
- When they have access to information and are protected from repression 
### Theories of Autocratic Institutionalization
- Autocrats want to hold power but they will most credibly signal that they will not monopolize power. Otherwise they risk rebellion
- Autocrats tie their own hands by creating institutions that constrain them 
- Powerful political actors are more likely to get behind a constrained autocrat 
- Dictators can shape the way that autocrats possess power and can undermine the power of the elite 

## "Mixed" Regimes 
- Authoritarian regimes with some democratic elements
- However those elements do not serve democratic purposes 
- Instead, they are often a tool for elite control or bargaining 
- These exist to fill a very specific purpose of focusing autocratic power 

### Mexico After The Revolution
- The revolution occurred under the banner of no reelection
- But the first president after the revolution tried to get reelected 
- To stabilize the country, elites need to know that the next leader wouldn't do the same 

### A Political Regime Under The PRI
- Dual challenges for the sitting president
	- Convince elites that he won't run for reelection
	- Stay in power without being the president 
- Solved the problem by creating the PRI
	- He could control who gets to be president **through the party**
	- The PRI would include all strong political actors
	- The party could **define rules for political competition**

### "Order And Progress"
- All relevant actors were assimilated into the party, often occupying positions of power within it
- This ensured the much-needed stability after the revolution
- But the shared hierarchy of the PRI also guaranteed the cohesion of regime elites 
- By the dictator empowering the elites and constraining his power, he is committing to sharing power and the elites can trust this agreement since the dictator is giving them the tools to punish him. Therefore, the elites know that if the dictator steps out of line, the elites can punish him 

### "Effective Suffrage, No Reelection"
- Mexico had a long history of leaders trying to perpetuate themselves in power
- Banning reelection addressed that problem 
- ==It also made it more appealing to be a part of the part==
- PRI members could reasonably expect to gain power without violence 
- The possibility that different factions within the party could get to power, it's a guarantee of some sort of selective incentive for members of the party especially the elite
- No reelection created a problem: how do you get autocrats to give up power?
- They had to be sure they would be safe after leaving office
- Mexican presidents had the right to choose successors that could protect them 

#### Why Did The PRI Rule For So Long
- Possible Explanations
	- Repression
	- Electoral fraud
	- Economic performance 

### Function of Elections in Autocracies
- The PRI stayed in power through electoral dominance
- Wide electoral margins signaled strength and demobilized the oppositions
- Lack of electoral fraud allowed information gathering 

### The PRI Advantage
- Given the PRI's dominance only ideologues joined the opposition
	- People who have an ideological commitment to issues. For example, the religious, conservative party in Mexico, for the PRI was famous for prosecuting the Catholics 
- Meanwhile, the PRI was able to be ideologically flexible and stay close to the average voter
- The PRI was a *catch all* party competing with niche ones
