## Socialism before Marx
- Socialism was an old idea when Marx came around
- People were already concerned with the social consequences of rapid industrialization
- **Phalanx**: small agricultural cooperatives with collective property 
- "What sovereign dies of hinger?" Charles Fourier, 1822

## Communism before Marx
- Some were dissatisfied with early socialist efforts
- "Property is theft" Proudhon, 1840
- Decried socialism as trying to fix something that was rotten
- They defined communism as the socialism of the working class

## The Communist Manifesto
- Marx wrote the Communist Manifesto as a program for the Communist League
- It served as the rallying document behind the communist movement 
- Frame the conflict as a fight between the **proletariat** and the **bourgeoisie** 

### Economy to Politics
- the economic system empowered the bourgeoisie by giving them wealth
- The bourgeoisie then uses its power to take over the state 
- The state becomes a tool for the production of capital and capitalism

### Politics to Economy
- A key concept in Marxism was class consciousness
- Workers with class consciousness recognized that a revolution was in their self-interest
- Workers without class consciousness were seduced by the goods an ideology of capitalism

### "All history has been a history of class struggles of struggles between exploited and exploiting"
- The manifesto had the promise of a society without exploitation
- The key was the shared ownership of the means of production (not equality) 
- Recognized this goal in political terms (definition of an enemy and justified revolution)

### Historical Antecedents of Communist Regimes
- **First International**: A congress uniting worker parties from across Europe
- **Russian Revolution**: Lenin used Marxism to mobilize the Bolsheviks (Russian Workers)

Communists believed that violent revolution was justified so in essence they were the only legitimate actor. Socialists believe that the system can be shaped from within the system and that revolution is not necessary

### The Soviet Model
- It was based on ==**state ownership t**he means of production==
- The state planned the economy
- Sought the rapid industrialization and specialization of the Soviet republics
- Funded by the collectivization of rural farms
	- Grain exports used to buy machinery and industrial equipment
	- Labor/food reallocation
	- But caused eight million deaths 
- Trotsky argued that state ownership was not communism 

### Growth During the Great Depression
- Economies across the world were affected by the Great Depression (1929-1933)
- But not the Soviet Union. It's economy was huge and domestically-oriented. This shieled it from international crisis. 

### Political Economy
- What can states do (or not do) to promote economic development and well being

## How did socialism first become mainstream in democratic countries?
Creating social services vs state taking control. 

### The Two Innovations of Socialism
1. An Ideological platform that appealed to the many
2. An organizational structure that incorporated the masses. (**Mass Base Parties**)

### The Socialist Agenda
- 8-work days
- Minimum wages and benefits
- Economic Intervention to the benefit of workers
	- Welfare state

### Socialism and Labor Movements
- Socialism resonated with the labor movements of the time
- Unions had been talking about worker's welfare but lacked a broader ideology
- Luddites: a secret worker organization protesting industrialization. Known for destroying equipment 
- Marxism provided people like the luddites with a more coherent (and appealing) ideology 
- Worker coordination across industries became easier with a shared ideology. It was about the proletariat as a whole. 

### Socialist Mass Parties
- Marxism provided the ideology while labor provided the organization
- Unions had an informal but still effective structure. Easy transition to political parties 
- In other cases, parties emerged independently but got the support of the unions

### Advantage of Mass Parties
- Socialist parties followed a model of mass participation
- People volunteered their time and money
- Useful for countering the financial advantage of other parties
- Invested in the ideological formation of membership
- This gave socialist parties stability and popular support 

### The Welfare State
- States that combine redistrubitive taxing and spending programs to provide basic needs and public goods like education, pensions, housing, and healthcare
- Usually combined with a strong "public sector" where states own companies in key sectors (e.g. energetic)
- Socialist pushed for the welfare state as a way to achieve redistribution without revolution
- But not inheritly left wing. German Chancellor Bismarck called it "practical Christianity" and the Nazi Party promoted it to gain supporters
