## Political Engagement

### Political and Support For Democracy
- The support for democracy depends on its performance
- But support for democracy also shapes democratic quality
- What other factors shape support for democracy
- **Three Levels of Engagement**
	- Parochial: people do not think of the government at all. They still think of public issues but only in terms of their community
	- Subject: people think of politics only in terms of what the government provides or takes away. The focus is on the "output" of politics. 
	- Participant: people engage actively with politics. They understand the processes underlying public decisions. 

#### Parochial Thinking & Democracy
- Inhibits political participation
- Limits cooperation with government
- Makes democratic accountability difficult 

#### Subject-Level Engagement
- **Clientelism**: A system where voters provide electoral support to candidates in return for some material benefit
- The benefit is usually economic, but may be jobs (patronage) or positions in power (prebendalism

#### Clientelism and Populism
- One element of populism is the commitment to working for the people
- Often comes in the shape of increased social welfare programs that target the most marginalized
- Clientelism or representation?

#### Benefits of High Political Cognition 
- Clientelism is more difficult when people value their vote highly 
- People value their vote when they understand the political process as a vehicle for influence
- How do we go from parochial to participant political cognition?
	- No amnesty for Bolsonaro in Brazil, people very strongly detested the idea of giving him amnesty due to Trump threatening tariffs 

### Three Paths to Participant Political Cognition
- Structural Variables: Economic and historical conditions that shape political cultures
- Contextual Variables: local organizations may present opportunities for participation
- Individual Variables: Elements that make people more susceptible to the influence of contextual and structural variables

### Spiral of Silence 
- People care a great deal about their interpersonal relationships, and not so much about politics
- People will not express their views if this means ostracization
- People conform to majority views to obtain a sense of belonging
- As a result, the majority never hears the minority views and the majority opinion becomes even more dominant 
- The Probability of ostracization depends on
	- The status of the dissenting person:
		- Are they leaders? 
		- Are they experts?
		- ==Individual Variables==
	- The characteristics of the group
		- Does the group value harmony
		- Is the group tolerant?
		- Is the group exposed to similar views elsewhere?
		- ==Structural and Contextual variables==

### Political Culture vs Public Opinion
- Political Culture: There are persistent national differences attributable to economic and historical conditions
- Public Opinion: Differences within national groups. They are more volatile and shaped by current events. 

#### Political Culture: Two Elements
**Traditional Vs Secular**
- Traditional Values: emphasize deference towards authority
- Secular: emphasize contestation and consensus
**Survival Vs Self-Expression**
- Survival: Favors insiders and emphasis on security
- Self-Expression: favors outsiders and emphasis on inclusion in the decision-making process 

### Political Culture
Modernization pushes societies towards secular and self expression values.
- self expression superfluous when in danger. Survival more important
- Development pushes people out of their communities and towards "outsiders"
- Development incentivizes personal efficacy and thus contestation of authority 
Modernization values are broadly correlated with better political engagement (turnout)
