Most Mexicans believe that the church should have some influence in the government. 
Catholicism is the most predominant religion within Mexico. 

## Church and State
- Why are the relations between Church and State *politically* important?
- Why is the Church different from other powerful groups?

**The Power of The Catholic Church**
- Unlike the New Spain state the Catholic Church actually penetrated most of the country
- State had to rely on Church
	- Tax collection
	- Administrative tasks
	- Keeping track of public opinion 
	- Building a nation
#### Social Organization of New Spain
- New Spain was highly hierarchical
- The racial background of a person determined their legal and social status
- Society was so fragmented that it made it hard for a shared identity to emerge that did not depend on the one common trait: Catholicism
#### Church and Identity Building
- The Church not only became a legitimate authority but also the shaper of cultural identity
- Gave people a shared narrative of their origins, and values
- Previously disconnected or hostile groups were assimilated into the Catholic culture

#### Church vs State
- The main obstacles to the centralization of power in Mexico was the Catholic Church
	- Liberals backed deposing church power while conservatives supported the Church
	- Conflict resulted in a civil war or the "Reform War"
- Allegiance to the church was not only self interested. It was also motivated by the ideological affinity
- A weak state made for a low-intensity conflict 
- Liberals won due to US backing 

### Separation of Church and State
- The new constitution ==ended the economic power== of the Church 
	- Church had to sell most of its land
	- Religious tithes were forbidden
	- Cemeteries and hospitals were taken away
	- Civil registry now fell on hands of state
- Transferring legitimacy of Church over to the State
- Taxes, developing military and police, increasing government presence 
- The constitution also dealt with the ==cultural hegemony== of the Church
	- Catholicism was no longer the official religion 
	- Other religions were now free to organize
	- Convents and cloisters were closed down
	- Religious holidays were no longer observed
	- All education was forced to be secular
	- All people were given the same rights and obligations 
- Mainly, the constitution dealt with the ==**political influence**== of the church
	- Clergymen now barred from political activity and could be prosecuted
	- Religious orders were banned
	- High ranking priests were exiled 
- The constitution also put the military under civilian control. 

### Porfiriato
- Porfirio Diaz took power after the end of the French occupation (and Juarez deposition)
- He continued to build the state
- His strategy favored cooperation
- Church regained some of it's power
- Diaz decided influence from Church was still a problem 
- Termed the term Mestizaje 

### Mestizaje
- The new identity could not be related to European heritage
- A natural solution was to look at the indigenous populations. Two key problems
	- Pre-Hispanic culture was devastated by the Spanish. Too little knowledge of it to serve as national identity
	- Indigenous populations presented an obstacle to Diaz' understanding of economic development
#### Rise of Mestizaje
- Instead of relying on pre-Hispanic culture, the new Mexican state pushed the mestizo as the ideal
- Mestizaje was the requirement for the incorporation of a capitalist mode of production
- "...all races improve by mixing" Alva - influential writer
- Almost omits the faults of the Racial question in America by being so broad that it includes everyone but people who didn't partake in mixing - were seen as the outsiders 
- The Cosmic Race - the race was superior to non-mixed races 
### The State and Difference
- The state suppresses difference to promote unity and centralization 
- Capitalism vs Race: Capitalism demands a single mode of production while indigenous populations demand respect to plural ways of life
- The state reinforces capitalism at the expense of indigenous demands
- The state at the service of mestizo nation 
#### The Mestizo State
- An interpretation of race in Mexico and more broadly. Three propositions 
1. Race is theory that organizes human difference based on a social hierarchy
2. Race is constructed through narratives
3. Race produces identity. It can create solidarity but, because it creates hierarchy, it segregates

Reflecting difficulty in unification and struggle to unify economically.
