- democratization comes in waves
- Three waves so far, followed by retractions
- How do we explain international patterns 

### Waves 1-3 
1. Democracy takes root in 19th century North America and West Europe
	1. Spread to 29 countries
	2. Trough between first and second world wars
2. Democratization following World War II 
	1. Establishment of democracy in former Axis powers
	2. India and other newly independent former colonies
	3. Spreads to 36 countries, some ex-colonies revert
3. Largest wave starting 1975
	1. Began with the end of military dictatorships across Europe and Latin America
	2. Culminated with of the Iron Curtain

### The Fall of The Berlin Wall
- The third wave of democratization was tied to the end of the Cold War
- Both superpowers were maintaining regimes that did not have the capability to support themselves
- Autocracies crumbled without the help of the USSR or broke down once the US was uninterested in combating communism 

### Autocrats and International Actors
- Foreign governments, financial institutions, and multilateral organizations all hold significant leverage over autocrats
- Autocrats who align with international powers gain political thinking and economic backing
	- Once US did not have to combat communism, authoritarian actors lost US backing, such as Pinochet who lost power once Carter and Reagan got into power 

### Foreign Led Regime Change
-  World powers have a long history of promoting regime change
	- At least 46% of countries in Latin America have been targeted by the US
	- Most effective in establishing new dictatorships, however
		- At least eight military dictatorships followed intervention in Latin America 
Why military dictatorships? Not only because of the strategic interests, but also because it's easier to give guns to the right group of people to sustain a democracy. 

### Why Intervene? 
- To secure strategic assets
	- Natural resources, shipping lanes, etc.
- Competition with rivals
- US, USSR, China, etc.
- Appease domestic constituencies. 
	- Economic elites, political leaders, etc. 

### A third "trough"
- A lot of international actors do not wish to spend time or money doing state building, rather they move the needle 
- Recent retreat of democracy 

### New Superpowers and Regime Change
- Overall, globalization has gien more power to countries interested in influencing domestic politics
- In some vases that means pressures for democracy, in other cases it means pressure for autocracy
- As more diverse actors start to gain access to international positions of power, ' waves' may become more uncommon
	- No reason to think that international involvement will lead to democratization

### Human Rights Regimes
- Superpowers are not the only people trying to influence domestic politics
- International human rights organizations, for example, also try to promote more inclusive political systems
- Can only influence domestic policy indirectly
