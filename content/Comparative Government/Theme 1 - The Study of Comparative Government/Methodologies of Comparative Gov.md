Epistemology of qualitative studies: Foundationalism
- the basis of our knowledge must be true '2+2=4'
	- basic beliefs that support our understanding
- build off your basic beliefs to ensure your knowledge is valid

### Qualitative Methodologies
- these want to get a deep and intimate understanding of one issue
- can intensely study one or a few cases
- the seek to incorporate the complexity of social phenomena
- foundationalist epistemology
- **ethnographic studies**

EXAMPLE 

Question: Why don't people give money to human rights organization (HROs)

Method: Focus Groups - ask Columbians about their opinions on why they don't support HROs

Finding: Most people don't support HROs because they don't think they are transparent 

Conclusion: HROs don't get popular support because people don't trust them. 

**But is this a convincing conclusion? What else is there to take into account. Is the conclusion justified**

Think Epistemologically
- Fundamentalist approach: people's reports may not qualify as basic beliefs
	- social concerns may push people to lie **or** people have limited mindfulness
- Generalizability problem: Do all Columbians think the same? (i.e. do the basic facts hold the same for everyone)

### Quantitative Studies - Reliablism
We cannot know a belief is true and justified until it is testified. But we can know that certain methods can reliably produce true and justified beliefs. Our focus should be on the way we produce our beliefs not the beliefs themselves. 
- Try to answer questions about degrees: e.g. "How much do financial crises affect presidential approval?" It's all about qualifying things of interest 
- Usually they posit correlations, collect relevant data, and analyze data using statistics
- ==Correlation==: statistical measure of how much different countries are related to each other. 

EXAMPLE

Question: Does watching news about violence make you more or less anxious?

Method: Have people watch many videos on terrorism and track their emotions/

Finding: People have more negative reactions than in the beginning. 

Conclusion: Prolonged exposure inures people. (people were less anxious)

Criticism: Inferring feelings just by looking at the face (measurement error). The experiment did not properly portray the news cyce.
