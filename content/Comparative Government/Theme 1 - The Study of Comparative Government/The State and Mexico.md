### States, Governments, and Regimes
- The state is the foundation of the political regime 
- the regime are the norms and laws that dictate access to government 
- Political regime in the US - we'll hold elections for congress and president. Those people will have certain capacities 
- **Government is different from the State**
	- government is people powered by the political regime 

> [!NOTE] What is the state?
> "the social order that has claimed the monopoly on the legitimate use of violence" - Max Weber, *Politics as a Vacation*

==legitimacy== is the degree to which people accept the authority of the state

==violence== refers to the actual physical harm (e.g. imprisonment) or simply the imposition of norms (e.g. taxation)

State definition in lecture - the political authority specifically. Legitimate use of violence. Decides who deserves to be punished - the lawful part. Possesses a more political side

## Who Did What? Mexico's Drug War

**Government**: President adopted a policy of military confrontation with the cartels. 

**Political Regime**: Gave the president access to the state, and the authority to dictate policy. 

**State**: Fights the war against the cartels. Collects taxes. 

## Why Do We Want A State?
- What does a world without a state?
	- Middle ages. People would fight each other because of the incentive to defect (Prisoner's dilemma)
- With a state, we get effective rule that protects the country and the population
- Social Contract - the state is legitimate because it gives people a way out of anarchy
- The state is able to punish those who don't oblige the social contract
- **Back to Mexico**
	- The Mexican state was a response to a period of domestic, anarchy, and foreign threats

## State Building in Mexico
- Two main obstacles to centralizing power
	- Power of the church and local leaders
	- Lack of common national identity 
- Government had to wrestle power away from others and force the Mexican identity
- Democratic governments tried and failed but authoritarian ones succeeded

## Paradox of The State
- The promise of the state is peace and order. However, the process to get there requires violent conflict 
- **The mixed constitution problem**
	- How do we maintain the effective rule and order associated with dictatorship but keep the pluralism associated with democracy

## What Causes Failed States - the example of Haiti
- Historical legacies such as colonialism
- Competition with foreign powers
- Competition with domestic actors
- Illegitimate weak, governments

Weak Haitian state fostered economic dependence which strained the ability of Haiti to determine it's own politics and economy.

## PRI and the Cartels
- before democratization, the PRI controlled the cartels for it's own purposes
- the cartels benefited from the PRI
- PRI served as third-party arbitrator between cartels 
- PRI held political power over the political regime, getting bad graces with the PRI meant bad news for the cartels. 
- This started to change after Mexico **democratized** 
	- Weaker gov because it is not unitary government, there is opposition

## Brokering Peace
*Commitment Problem*: No one can show a real intention to abide by peace agreements because there is no third actor to punish those who renege.
