
### Socioeconomic Marginalization of Women
- Homes with a female occupant are less likely to cover basic necessities (sewage, clean water, etc.) 36.5 vs 6.4
- Women are 30% less likely to have access to education

### Violence Against Women
- 54% of women report being physically or sexually abused at work
	- 44% in the US
	- VAW (Violence Against Women) Index
- This is bad, but in the world this is average

## The Puzzles
- Mexico leads the world in representation and violence against women
- **How did women achieve representation despite marginalization?**
- **Why has representation not reflected better conditions for women?**

### Levels of Representation
- **Symbolic Representation:** representatives acknowledge the members of a group and the legitimacy of their claims. Acts more of a lip service, is important, but still lip service.
- **Descriptive Representation:** Representatives have a shared background with members of a group
- **Substantive Representation**: Representatives push for policies in line with the interests of he group. 

### What Has Driven Political Inclusion?
- Gender quotas
- About 52% of countries have gender or ethnic quotas
- In Latin America, candidate quotas are the norm and have helped descriptive representation
	- Observed in the US as Affirmative Action 

#### The Origins of Quotas
- Quotas often emerge as a result of activism
- Activists lobbied, litigated and formed cross-partisan coalitions to demand them
	- ==Pluralism in action==
- "We realized we could not advance together unless we were together...My first lesson was working across parties" - Maria Elena Chapa, PRI congresswoman 

#### The Limits of Identity
- Quotas faced steep opposition from the women in the PAN (conservative party)
- "We have always opposed bills that establish discrimination. We do not believe being a woman qualifies one for office" Maria Cristi, Chilean Congresswoman
- Rarely are people's priorities fully determined by one aspect

### Intersectionality and The Fight For Descriptive Representation 
- Female legislators are under the pressure of many different forces
- This raises the costs of coordination. Women need to reconcile contrasting preferences
- **Intersectionality**: People belong to different groups. Each group membership imbues them with different interests. As a result, everyone has a unique configuration of interests
	- Wrong to assume that sharing a common identity is enough for coordination. 

### Intersectionality in Mexico
- The agenda of female legislators is shaped by many identities
	- Socio-economic
	- Religion
	- Partisan
	- Gender
- Hence, they often fail to coordinate around shared priorities 
 
### Sources of Substantive Representation
- Left parties have traditionally advocated for the inclusion of marginalized groups
- But their results are lackluster
- Little advantage in terms of substantive representation 

### Why Are Progressive Parties Bad At Producing Substantive Representation 
- Party discipline limits substantive representation despite descriptive representation 
	- "Meetings happen late at night at hotels, so I cannot join them without putting my reputation at risk" Argentine Deputy 
- In catch-all parties, descriptive representation is cheaper to provide 
	- Intersectionality makes gendered issues less electorally profitable 
- **Median Voter Theory** - best bet is to not commit to anything for parties

### Quotas And Substantive Representation 
- Intersectionality is a challenge, but it can be addressed 
- Build broad coalitions by extending similar advantages to the marginalized
- But can there be unintended consequences? 

### From Descriptive to Substantive Representation
- Since 1996, Mexico has passed legislation to the benefit of women
	- Labor: Paid maternity leave, childcare subsidies, no limitations on work (2000)
	- Violence: Legislation to protect physical integrity (2008) and prevent human human trafficking (2007, 2009)
	- Domestic Labor: rights for those working in domestic labor
- Quotas have not just been symbolic
