## The Human Rights Gap
- The more democratic, the more human rights you have. **however this is not true in every instance**
	- South Africa, Israel, El Salvador, Mexico, Armenia, Turkey, Indonesia, India, Columbia 
- Democratic functions can work well enough to kick people out of power but not enough to protect people

# What is Repression?
*demobilizing the opposition

### Repression Defined
- Repression is **politically defined**
- It seeks to make dissent costly
- Different from exploitation or human rights 
	- This definition is devoid of any sense of morality and agnostic. Does not have personal opinions involved. For example - Germany banning any symbols of Nazi Germany is a repressive action
"Repression is any measure that raises the contender's cost of collective action" - Charles Tilly 

### Forms Of Repression
- Repression is not just about police beating up dissidents
- Can also be a legal system that denies meaningful access to power, justice, or economic activity
- It can also be the *threat* of punishment
- All of these actions raise the cost of collective action

### The Puzzle of Democratic Repression
Democracies have a lot of incentives to not repress
- Repressive leaders can be prosecuted 
- Political mobilization can make repression costly
- Repressive leaders can be voted out of office
- Why then does repression persist? 

### Repression as Lack of Control
- Sometimes repression is not about the politicians' incentive to repress
- Coercive agents may repress because its the easier option
- Example of **principle agent problem** 

### Principle Agent Problem
- Principle: the boss that has the authority to define policy
- Agent: those who carry out the policy
- Why would the principal defer to the agent?
	- Expertise, resources, outsource responsibility, etc. 
- There is a disparity between the interests of the agent and the principal 

### Repression as a Principal-Agent Problem
- Government may have an interest in curtailing dissent
- But may not have an interest or the capacity to do oversight
- **Under no oversight,** agents choose the cheapest option for curtailing dissent: repression
- Principals that have the incentives not to repress, may nevertheless produce repressive outcomes 

### Common Agency Problems
- Agents may have many principals
- These principals may have different interests
- The agent have a change to deviate by exploiting coordination issues among principals

### Democratic Representations and Common Agency Problems
- Elected officials have many principals (the public)
- Officials have a chance to deviate by exploiting coordination issues among the problem
- Here, agent deviation is **not about a lack of oversight**, it is about a lack of agreement between principals 

### Repression and Democratic Repression
- Abusive leaders can, in principle be ousted of office
- But public opinion is often uninterested in punishing abuse
