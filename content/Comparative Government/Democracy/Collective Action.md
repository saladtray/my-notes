### Collective Action and Democracy
- Democracy needs collective action to work 
- Collective Action: groups of people work together to achieve a common goal
- Democratic political engagement, like elections and protests, necessitates group action 

### The Challenge of Collective Action
- Democracy is hard because people have an incentive to free ride
- **Free Ride**: to reap the benefits that collective action provides after others have put in the effort to mobilize
- Elections and activism are susceptible to free ride problem 

#### Why Do People Free Ride?
- People free ride because they can expect to receive the benefits of collective action even when staying home
- We call goods that benefit the community "**public goods**"
- *Nonexcludability* defines public goods. If they are provided at all, everyone gets them, regardless if they "paid" for it. 
- Democratic public goods: accountability policy change, human rights 

### Selective Incentives as a Solution
- Selective Incentives are private goods that allow a group to reward those who contributed to those who contributed to the groups goal
- Economic benefit, reputational gains, decision-making power, etc.
- Groups that provide selective incentives can make people engage in collective action 
- Not all organizations are equally able to provide selective incentives
- They may lack money or power to distribute

### Social Movements
- They offer broad appeals, often based on a political identity
- A lack of organization prevents the use of selective incentives
- Institutional inclusion requires moderation, so social movements are unlikely to achieve it
	- If they moderate they will struggle to provide broad appeals ("sellouts")

### Social Movement Success
- The success of social movements depend on finding the right appeal (i.e. the right message)
- But the conditions must also provide a good chance of success
	- i.e. people participate more when they think they will reach a critical mass or turning point
- We refer to these conditions as the political opportunity structure
	- It shapes the perception of the likelihood of success
	- Can be just be about anything, depends on the observers

### Interest Groups
- Focus on one issue area that benefits a smaller group of people (their supporters)
	- Limits collective incentives/appealing potential
- They offset lower collective incentives with selective ones
	- i.e. pay their members for their services 
- Emergence is not dependent on an ideal
- Their moderation allows them to operate in the institutional space but are not restricted to having to win office, necessitating less compromise 

### Political Parties
- They ***usually*** are the most moderated because they need to operate within institutions
- Cannot push one issue without having to consider others
	- Remember the median voter theorem
- They make up for the lack of broad appeals with an extensive capacity for selective incentives 


|                        | Social Movements | Interest Groups                        | Political Parties                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vehicle of Recruitment | broad appeals    | broad appeals and selective incentives | selective incentives                                     |
| Level of Action        | radical          | policy                                 | discourse                                                |
| Term of Action         | short-term/waves | long-term                              | short-term OR long-term (institutionalization dependent) |

### The Effect of Collective Action
- Political culture produces engagement - **collective action can do the same**
- People join interest groups for the selective incentive but then buy into the political agenda
- Collective action creates "issue publics" that can later develop more general political interests

Has to be some equality in political action, via economics and ability to access politics, for pluralism to actually work. Economic requirement for democracy to work, inequality is the enemy of that.
