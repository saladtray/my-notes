- Legislative efficacy drops, on average, 36% during divided government 
- Tyranny and anarchy on repeat
	- But how much can be attributed to the US interference 
- Economic factors supporting democracy weren't so strong 


> "Political Regimes, however one thinks about them, are complex. They combine many institutional features that can have emergent effects and that may work at cross-purposes"

### Three Requirements for Presidential Systems

1. Executives are popular elected
2. They cannot be dismissed for political reasons
3. Presidents direct the government they appoint 

### Presidential Democracies
- The three state powers reside within different government institutions 
- Executive is not responsive to the legislature
- This creates a **'double legitimacy'** problem 
	- there is no mechanism to solve conflicts between these two powers
	- the legislative and executive can present themselves as representative of popular will 
	- no good way to settle dispute between the two
	- leads to inefficiency and anarchy
### Divided Government and Party Intransigence
- Party Discipline: the extent to which party leaders can force party members to behave in a certain manner 
- When different parties control different branches of government we get gridlock
	- Mitch McConnel goes on to say that republicans try to make Democratic presidents seem inefficient and uncapable 

## Electoral Rules and Parties
- We use them to decide who to translate votes into seats (electoral rules)
- Many decisions: size of district, runoff vs instant runoff, public vs private funding, etc.
- Most important we distinguish between ==proportional representation== and ==plurality rule==

### Plurality Rule
- Observed in the US
- Plurality rule minimizes the number of parties
- Politicians know that going against the party line can risk their careers
- Its consequences on inclusion depend on district size (issue of gerrymandering)

### Proportional Representation
- one vote per person, many winners
- Voted candidates share power
- A popular party cannot monopolize power
- Smaller parties still get a say in government

### PR and Party Discipline
- In theory, PR allows politicians to switch parties
- Party switching undermines elite control over parties
	- Also undermines **efficacy**
- In the extreme proportional representation fragments power. Incentivizes corruption as the main avenue to get things done
	- 5 out of 6 of the last Peruvian presidents are now in prison, fugitives, or dead
- You can still have party discipline under PR
- Cooperation becomes even more untenable under those conditions

# Characteristics of Parliaments
- No separation of powers between executive and legislative
- The government (i.e. the executive) depends on the legislative
- The government is led by the Prime Minister who is a member of parliament

### Legislative Responsibility in Practice
A legislative majority has the right to remove the government without cause
- Question Time: Members of Parliament hold prime minister accountable for actions of government
- Censure Motion: Formal process by which members of parties signal disapproval of government
- Vote of Confidence: A legislative vote where the government must resign if it does not win the majority. Can be initiated by the government or the minority. 

### Legislative Responsibility In Practice
- Confidence votes means there is always a majority when government exists
- No majority = no government
	- Creates needs for unstable coalitions
- Gridlocks can be just as common as in presidential systems

## Democracy as a System
- No institution can guarantee specific outcomes
- Outcomes depend on other aspects of the political system (e.g. party discipline)
- System: All parts are interconnected, changing one changes all.
